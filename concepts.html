ACTING FUNDAMENTALS

Key Acting Concepts

Understanding acting terminology and techniques helps performers create believable characters and meaningful performances.

Character Development

Character development is the process of understanding and creating a believable character through their personality, background, emotions, and relationships.

Actors explore what their characters want, why they want it, and how they respond to different situations.

Script Analysis

Script analysis involves carefully examining a script to understand the story, character relationships, conflicts, and important events.

This process helps actors make informed choices about how to portray their characters.

Essential Acting Terminology

Objective

An objective is what a character wants to achieve during a scene or performance.

Motivation

Motivation explains why a character behaves in a particular way.

Subtext

Subtext is the underlying meaning or emotion behind a character's spoken words.

Blocking

Blocking refers to planned actor movements and positions during a performance.

Improvisation

Improvisation involves creating dialogue or actions spontaneously without a fully prepared script.

Continuity

Continuity means maintaining consistent actions, movements, and details across different takes during filming.

Emotional Preparation

Emotional preparation helps actors understand and communicate a character's emotional experiences.

Actors may use imagination, character research, and scene circumstances to create believable emotional responses.

Collaboration in Acting

Acting is a collaborative art form involving performers, directors, writers, and production teams.

Communication, preparation, flexibility, and teamwork contribute to successful productions.
